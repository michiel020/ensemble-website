How it works

What working with us looks like

Getting an initiative off the ground isn't a one-person job. Too much is happening at once: building and bringing the organization along, customers and stakeholders. Some friction along the way comes with the territory. Usually it's a sign that something real is at stake.

And when one of us is stuck in a steering committee, the other keeps things moving. That's the difference between two people and one interim manager.

  1. Week 1

    We dive in

    No hiring process, no onboarding, no quarter lost to getting started. We talk to your stakeholders, read up on what's already there and map out where things are stuck. By the end of that week, we'll tell you what we think needs to happen.

  2. From week 2

    Building and validating at the same time

    Every two weeks we show you what works: a validated assumption, a prototype, a page that's live. If there's ever nothing to show, we talk about why. That conversation gets you more than a progress report. You don't need to look over our shoulder; we'll bring you in when it matters.

  3. Every quarter

    A milestone goes live

    Not one big delivery at the end, but something running and in use every quarter. That changes the dynamic: the steering committee and partners see progress instead of plans. Discussions are about what works instead of what might work, and the next step is easier to agree on together.

  4. After that

    It keeps running without us

    We build something durable, stable, and scalable. Once the foundation is laid, we hand over to the people who'll run the initiative for the long haul, with the team, the rhythm, and the ownership already in place.

Our rhythm: a check-in every week, a demo of what works every two weeks, a step back to the goals every month, and a milestone live every quarter.

What we're proud of

The Energy Hubs Knowledge Platform

In nine weeks, from scattered knowledge to the national go-to place where parties find each other to get energy hubs off the ground.

Businesses that want to grow or go green keep running into a congested power grid. An energy hub is often the answer, but in 2025 there was nowhere to see what already existed, what worked, and who to call. The knowledge was scattered across reports, email threads, and one-off initiatives.

Together with the Dutch Ministry of Economic Affairs and Climate Policy, RVO, Netbeheer Nederland, and PVB, we built that place.

Phase 1

January — April 2025

Unlocking the knowledge

After more than forty conversations with government bodies, grid operators, market players, and energy hubs, we drew up a development agenda. Nine weeks later the first version was live: a step-by-step plan, a knowledge base, tools, and the first real-world cases.

Phase 2

May — December 2025

Building out what users asked for

A release every quarter, each one based on user feedback. The initiative radar grew from zero to over two hundred registered hubs. We added an experts section, a glossary, and more tools and cases; together the knowledge base and tools grew to 125 items.

Phase 3

From January 2026

Scaling up and connecting

The platform is now part of PVB/TKI Urban Energy. Six working groups are tackling what's holding scale-up back: cyber resilience, cost benchmarking and the business case, insurability, model contracts, the IT landscape, and financing. And a new audience came on board: municipalities.
